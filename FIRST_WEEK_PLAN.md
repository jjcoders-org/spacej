
# SpaceJ — First Week Execution Plan

Start: 17 November 2026

## Day 1 — Establish the foundation

- [ ] Complete the repository documentation.
- [ ] Create a technical research workspace.
- [ ] Identify existing aerospace engineering software.
- [ ] Begin the industry landscape study.
- [ ] Record all findings with references.

Deliverable: A properly documented engineering project.

## Day 2 — Understand the industry

Study companies and organisations across:

- Launch vehicles.
- Satellite manufacturing.
- Spacecraft systems.
- Propulsion.
- Mission software.
- Ground infrastructure.
- Space data.

For each company, record its products, customers, technology, and business model.

Deliverable: An aerospace industry map.

## Day 3 — Study existing engineering technology

Investigate existing mission-design and simulation software.

Understand:
- What it does.
- How it works.
- Which technology it uses.
- Who uses it.
- What limitations it has.
- What opportunities might exist.

Deliverable: An existing-tools comparison.

## Day 4 — Discover real problems

Identify at least ten potential aerospace users or research groups.

Prioritise:
- University satellite teams.
- Aerospace researchers.
- Small satellite companies.
- Mission-analysis engineers.
- Ground-segment engineers.

Attempt three genuine conversations.

Deliverable: Initial user-discovery evidence.

## Day 5 — Evaluate problems

Compare candidate problems using:

- Severity.
- Frequency.
- Existing alternatives.
- Cost of current workarounds.
- Potential customers.
- Technical feasibility.
- Strategic value.

Deliverable: A shortlist of up to three problems.

## Day 6 — Select the first problem

Select one problem based on evidence.

Prepare:
- Problem statement.
- Target user.
- Existing workflow.
- Proposed solution.
- Technical approach.
- Validation method.
- Success criteria.

Deliverable: First product specification.

## Day 7 — Begin implementation

Build the smallest working experiment.

Prioritise correct calculations, reproducibility, and a demonstrable result.

Deliverable: First engineering prototype or validated technical experiment.

## Weekly review

At the end of the week:

1. What did we learn?
2. What did we build?
3. Which assumptions were disproved?
4. What did potential users tell us?
5. What should we do next?

Never claim a milestone is complete without evidence.
