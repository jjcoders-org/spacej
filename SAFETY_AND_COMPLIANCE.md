
# SpaceJ — Safety and Compliance

SpaceJ recognises that aerospace engineering can involve serious risks to people, property, the environment, and other space users.

## Current permitted project scope

At the foundation stage, our activities are limited to:

- Research.
- Software development.
- Mathematics.
- Non-operational simulation.
- Public-data analysis.
- Educational engineering.

This repository does not authorise physical rocket or propulsion experiments.

## Physical engineering

Any physical engineering work must first undergo appropriate:

- Risk assessment.
- Professional technical review.
- Facility assessment.
- Qualified supervision.
- Regulatory assessment.
- Safety planning.

No hazardous experiments at home, school, or improvised facilities.

## Indian space-sector requirements

Study:

Indian Space Policy 2023:
https://www.isro.gov.in/media_isro/pdf/IndianSpacePolicy2023.pdf

IN-SPACe:

The exact regulatory requirements depend on the activity. Obtain qualified advice and the necessary authorisations before undertaking regulated space activities.

## Software limitations

- Clearly label simulations.
- Document assumptions.
- Record data sources.
- Validate calculations.
- Document known limitations.
- Never describe an early prototype as flight-qualified software.
- Do not provide unvalidated operational flight or collision-avoidance recommendations.

## Data and intellectual property

- Respect third-party software licences.
- Use lawful public data.
- Never publish confidential or restricted information.
- Obtain permission before publishing interview material.

## Organisational responsibilities

Before agreements, investment, contracts, or physical programmes, involve appropriate adult oversight where legally required and qualified legal, financial, engineering, and safety professionals.

## Stop-work policy

Any contributor may stop work when they identify a safety, legal, or security concern.

The concern must be reviewed before the affected work resumes.
